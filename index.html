About

I am a researcher and software engineer from Bangladesh. My main research question is whether the safety of AI systems holds for everyone who uses them. My benchmark jbladder measures how open language models respond to harmful requests in seven languages, and it found that safety tracks the languages a model was aligned in, not how much data a language has. Bangla, my first language, was among the least protected.

My other line of work is applied cryptography. I am a co-author of HELO, a lightweight cryptographic system for secure peer-to-peer data transfer between IoT devices, which grew out of my undergraduate thesis. I am now working on a post-quantum pairing protocol for such devices.

Alongside research, I have taught Algorithms, Operating Systems and Computer Graphics at BRAC University, built AI-powered software in production as a freelance developer, and co-founded volunteer initiatives in education and community health.

Interests: AI safety and security · LLM red-teaming and evaluation · multilingual NLP · applied cryptography and IoT security · trustworthy machine learning · computational biology.

AI safetySole authorPreprint-ready

jbladder: a cross-lingual jailbreak robustness benchmark for open LLMs

2026 · Code: github.com/Nuharian/jailbreak-robustness-ladder (MIT licence)

A benchmark that asks whether a language model's refusal of harmful requests survives translation. It introduces the comprehension-controlled attack-success rate (cc-ASR), which separates genuine jailbreaks from answers a model gives because it did not understand the request, using a three-way judge (refuse / comply / evade).

  • Seven languages (Hindi, Bangla, Urdu, Arabic, Chinese, Spanish, French), four open-weight models and six conditions: 19,800 judged responses, run on a single 8 GB laptop GPU with no paid APIs.
  • cc-ASR of 0.86 in Bangla, 0.79 in Urdu and 0.76 in Hindi, against 0.31 in English.
  • Safety follows alignment, not resource level: Spanish and French held up on multilingually aligned Qwen models (0.01–0.12) but reached about 0.80 on the English-centric SmolLM2. And capability is not safety: Chinese was understood well, yet its cc-ASR was 0.51.
  • The judge was validated on 114 natively read labels (0.84 accuracy). That check caught a tokenisation defect that would otherwise have reversed the Chinese result.

HELO Cryptography: a lightweight cryptographic system for IoT security in peer-to-peer data transmission

2026 · Third of six authors · arXiv:2605.03948 · Undergraduate thesis group, supervised by Dr. Muhammad Iqbal Hossain

A hybridised lightweight cryptographic system for secure data transfer between constrained IoT devices, composed from standard primitives (ECDH key agreement, HKDF-SHA3, ChaCha20-Poly1305, ECDSA) and evaluated empirically. Public preprint, currently under review.

A content-filtering “middleman” proxy for social media

2026 · Code available on request

An inline moderation proxy between a user and a social platform: layered heuristics, a custom text classifier and a CLIP-based vision channel produce allow / blur / block decisions under a fail-soft policy, where a wrong blur is recoverable in one click.

  • F1 0.82 (precision 0.75, recall 0.90) on about 11,000 held-out posts from five real-world corpora; benign hard-removals cut by about 46%.
  • Adversarial obfuscation (leetspeak, spacing, vowel-dropping) lowered recall to 0.76; training-time augmentation restored it to 0.93, and 0.88 on an unseen attack family.
